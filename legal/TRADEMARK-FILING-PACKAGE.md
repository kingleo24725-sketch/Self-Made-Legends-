# Trademark Filing Package — SELF-MADE LEGENDS

**Prepared 27 August 2026 for Self-Made Legends LLC (Missouri).**

**This is not legal advice.** I am not a lawyer and I cannot file this for you.
A trademark application ends with a declaration signed **under penalty of
perjury** — only the applicant or their attorney can sign it, and it has to be
your payment card. What this document does is answer every question the form
asks, so filing is twenty minutes of typing instead of two weeks of reading.

---

## STOP — two things must happen before you file

### 1. Sign the IP assignment first

This is not optional and it is not paperwork for later.

The application asks **who owns the mark**. If the LLC applies for a mark it
does not legally own yet, the application can be held **void from the
beginning** — not fixable by amendment, not refundable. You would lose the
fee and the filing date.

Right now the brand was built by you personally. `legal/IP-ASSIGNMENT-DRAFT.md`
is the document that moves it into the LLC. **Sign that, dated before the
filing date.** Then the LLC is the honest answer to "who owns this."

### 2. Search before you spend

Fees are **non-refundable**. If someone already registered a confusingly
similar mark for clothing, you pay $350, wait five months, and get refused.

Search free at **https://tmsearch.uspto.gov** (no account needed).

I could not run this search for you — the USPTO search system is blocked from
where I run. But I did find two things in ordinary web results that you must
check by hand, because they are the most likely reason this gets refused:

| What I found | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **LEGENDS** — Reg. No. 6164171, The Legends Brand, Inc., registered 2020, **clothing** | A registered one-word mark for the same goods. An examiner may say SELF-MADE LEGENDS is confusingly similar. This is your #1 risk. |
| **SELF MADE FAMILY** — Serial 86334585, clothing (shirts, hats, sweatshirts) | Same "SELF MADE" opening, same goods. Check whether it is still live. |

Neither is a verdict. "LEGENDS" is a weak, widely-used word and there are many
coexisting `___ LEGENDS` clothing marks. But **this is the exact question worth
paying an attorney one hour to answer before you spend $700.**

**Searches to run, in this order:**

1. `tmsearch.uspto.gov` → search `legends` → filter International Class **025** → filter **LIVE** only. Read every result.
2. Same, class **041**.
3. Search `self made` in class 025 and 041.
4. Google `"self made legends" clothing` and check Instagram, TikTok, Etsy, Amazon. Someone selling under this name without registering still has **common-law rights** in their area and can block you there.

Write down every live mark you find that is close. That list is what you hand
the attorney.

## What you are filing

**Two separate applications is wrong. One application, two classes, is right** —
same form, you just add a second class.

| | Class | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Clothing** | **025** | Hoodies, tees, hats — the physical garments |
| **The game** | **041** | The online game people play in a browser |

### Why 041 and not "software"

You said clothing and software. On the clothing side that is simple. On the
software side the class depends on **what the customer actually gets**:

| If the customer... | Class |
|---|---|
| Downloads an app onto their phone or computer | **009** |
| Plays it in a browser, downloads nothing | **041** (entertainment service) |
| Logs into a business tool in a browser | **042** (SaaS) |

Your game runs at `web-production-576d9.up.railway.app` in a browser. Nobody
downloads anything. **That is Class 041.**

This matters because **you generally cannot move goods to a different class
after you file.** File the game in Class 009 today and the examiner refuses it
as unsupported — $350 gone, and you refile from scratch. If you later ship a
real downloadable app, you add Class 009 then, as a new application.

**Optional third class:** if a downloadable app is genuinely coming in the next
2–3 years, you can add Class 009 now on an intent-to-use basis for $350 and
lock in today's priority date. Only do it if you mean it — an intent-to-use
filing requires a *bona fide* intention, and you have three years to prove it.

## Exact answers for every field

Open **https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/apply** → **Trademark Center**. Create
a USPTO.gov account with two-factor. Choose **Base application**.

### Applicant

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Applicant type | **Limited Liability Company** |
| Name | `Self-Made Legends LLC` — **exactly** as it reads on your Missouri Secretary of State filing. Not "Self Made Legends LLC". Not "SML LLC". Copy it character for character. |
| State of organization | `Missouri` |
| Domicile address | The LLC's **actual principal place of business** — a street address, not a PO Box |
| Email | Use a real address you will still read in five years. `ceo@selfmadelegendsz.com` is fine. |

> **Read this before you type the address.** The domicile address becomes
> **public and permanently searchable**. If your principal place of business is
> your home, your home address goes on the public record. The USPTO allows a
> petition to hide it, but the standard is strict and it is a hassle after the
> fact. You **cannot** just put a mailbox address — the domicile has to be the
> real principal place of business, and misstating it is a false statement on a
> sworn form. If this bothers you, sort out a genuine commercial address for the
> LLC *before* filing, or ask the attorney to handle it. Don't solve it by
> typing something untrue.

### The mark itself

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Mark format | **Standard character** |
| Mark text | `SELF-MADE LEGENDS` |

**Why standard character and not your logo:** a standard character mark protects
**the words in any font, any colour, any styling**. It is the broader, stronger
right. A design mark protects only that specific artwork — change the logo and
the protection weakens.

File the words now. The seal and crown can be filed later as a separate design
mark ($350 per class again) once the brand is earning. If you only ever file
one thing, file the words.

**Include the hyphen.** You have been consistent about `Self-Made Legends`
everywhere — website, terms, licence, garments. The application should match how
you actually use it.

### Goods — Class 025

**Use the built-in picker, do not type your own description.** Typing your own
costs **$200 extra per class**. Picking from the USPTO's pre-approved list costs
nothing.

In Trademark Center, search the ID Manual picker for each of these and select
the pre-approved entry:

```
Hooded sweatshirts
Sweatshirts
T-shirts
Hats
Jackets
Sweatpants
```

Only list what you will actually sell. Listing goods you never sell is a
liability — a competitor can later cancel your registration for those items, and
in a bad case attack the whole thing. Six honest entries beat forty hopeful ones.

### Goods — Class 041

Search the picker for:

```
Entertainment services, namely, providing online video games
```

If that exact entry isn't in the picker, `Providing online computer games` is
the usual alternative. **Whatever you choose, pick it from the list.** The moment
you type into the free-form box it is +$200.

### Filing basis — read carefully, this is where people commit fraud by accident

Two options per class:

- **Section 1(a) — Use in Commerce.** You are *already* selling this across
  state lines. Requires a specimen now.
- **Section 1(b) — Intent to Use.** You genuinely intend to. No specimen now;
  you file one later when you launch.

**Claiming use in commerce when you are not actually selling can void the
registration for fraud — years later, at the worst possible moment.** This is
the single most common way small brands destroy their own trademark.

My read of where you actually stand:

| Class | Basis | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **025 Clothing** | **1(b) Intent to Use** | You have no Stripe links live and have not shipped a garment. You are not in use yet. |
| **041 Game** | **1(b) Intent to Use** — unless you can point to real players | The game is live, but "use in commerce" for a service means actually rendering it to the public across state lines. If it is you and a handful of test accounts, that is not it yet. |

**Intent-to-use does not cost you priority.** When it registers, your rights date
back to **today's filing date**, not to when you eventually sold the first
hoodie. You get the queue position now and prove use later. For a brand that
hasn't launched, 1(b) is not the weaker choice — it is the correct one.

## Specimens — the part that trips up every clothing brand

You don't need these to file on 1(b). You need them **later**, and getting them
wrong costs you months. Start collecting them now.

### What the USPTO refuses

- Printful/Printify **mockups** — a design rendered onto a stock photo
- Anything where the logo **floats** on the garment or has pixelation around it
- Digital renderings, printer's proofs, draft web pages
- A picture of just the artwork with no garment

They are actively looking for these and they refuse them routinely.

### What the USPTO accepts — Class 025

- A **photograph of a real garment** showing the **sewn-in neck label** with
  SELF-MADE LEGENDS on it
- A **hangtag attached to the actual garment** — photograph the tag *on* the
  shirt, not the tag alone
- A live **product page with a working Buy button**, showing the mark, the
  price, and the ability to actually order

**Action:** when your Printful sample order arrives, photograph the neck label
and the hangtag on the real garment, in real light, and save those files. That
photo is worth $350.

### What the USPTO accepts — Class 041

A screenshot of the live game page showing **SELF-MADE LEGENDS** and clearly
showing the game being offered — the mark has to be associated with the service
on the page, not just sitting in the footer.

Save the **URL and the date** with every screenshot. The form asks for both.

## Do you need a lawyer?

**Not legally.** You are US-based, so you can file yourself. Foreign applicants
are required to use one; you are not.

**Practically — for this specific filing, I'd pay for one hour.** Not for the
typing. For one question: *given that LEGENDS is registered for clothing, does
SELF-MADE LEGENDS clear?* That is a judgement call about likelihood of confusion,
it is the whole ballgame here, and it is the one thing neither you nor I can
answer by reading a database.

Many trademark attorneys do a **flat-fee search + filing**. Ask for that price
up front.

**Do not use a trademark filing mill** — the cheap online "we'll file your
trademark for $99 + fees" services. They type your form and disappear the moment
an office action arrives, which is exactly when you need someone. If you want
help, hire a licensed attorney; if you want to save money, file it yourself
properly using this document. The middle option is the worst of both.

## After you file

### Watch for the scam mail — it is coming

The moment you file, your name, address, and mark become **public**. Within
weeks you will receive official-looking letters and emails with names like
*"Patent & Trademark Office"*, *"Trademark Registration and Monitoring Office"*,
*"WTMR"*, or *"US Trademark Compliance"*, demanding several hundred dollars for
"registration", "publication", or "monitoring".

**They are all scams.** Every single one.

The rules:

- The USPTO **never sends paper invoices** demanding payment.
- Real USPTO email comes only from **@uspto.gov**.
- Every real fee is paid **only** at **uspto.gov**, logged into your own account.
- If a notice arrives any other way, it is not from the government. Bin it.

Some of these look extremely convincing — official seals, correct serial number,
correct filing date, because all of that is public. **Correct details prove
nothing.**

### Track it yourself

Your application gets a **serial number**. Check it at
**https://tsdr.uspto.gov** — free, no login.

Check it **once a month**. If an office action issues, you have **3 months** to
respond (extendable once, 3 more months, $125). **Miss it and the application
goes abandoned.** The USPTO emails you, but email fails; the monthly check is the
backstop.

### Rough timeline

| Stage | When |
|---|---|
| First examination | ~5 months after filing |
| Published for opposition (if approved) | ~2 months later, 30-day window for others to object |
| Notice of Allowance (intent-to-use) | ~2 months after publication |
| You file the Statement of Use | Whenever you're actually selling |
| **Registered** | **~14 months** typical, longer with an office action |

This is slow and that is normal. Your priority date is the day you file, so the
clock protecting you starts now, not at the end.

## Using ™ and ® correctly

| Symbol | When | Your situation |
|---|---|---|
| **™** | Any time, no permission needed, for goods | **Use it today** — on garments, tags, the website |
| **℠** | Same, for services | Optional for the game |
| **®** | **Only after the USPTO registers it** | **Do not use this yet.** Using ® on an unregistered mark is unlawful and can be used against you. |

Right now: `Self-Made Legends™`. The day the registration certificate arrives,
switch to `®` and update the website footer, terms, and licence.

## What about Missouri state registration?

$50 per class, Chapter 417 RSMo, Missouri Secretary of State.

**It is cheap and it is nearly worthless to you.** It covers Missouri only. It
does not stop a company in Texas, it does not help with Amazon or Instagram
takedowns, and it gives you no rights in federal court. You sell online, to the
whole country — the state registration protects the one place you were already
protected by common law.

**Skip it. Put the $100 toward the federal filing.**

## What this actually buys you

Be clear about what registration does and does not do, because you have already
paid for a website's worth of legal language that mostly buys standing rather
than protection:

**What a federal registration gives you:**

- Nationwide rights from your filing date, in every state, including ones you have never sold in
- The presumption you own it — the other side has to disprove it, not you
- The right to sue in federal court
- The ability to record it with **US Customs**, which is how counterfeit shipments get seized at the border
- The thing Amazon Brand Registry, Instagram, TikTok, Etsy and Shopify actually require before they will take down someone copying you
- Something you can license, sell, or leave to your daughter — a trademark, unlike a copyright, **never expires** as long as you keep using it and keep filing

**What it does not do:**

- It does not stop anyone. It gives you the tools to stop them. Someone still has to send the letter.
- It does not cover the logo artwork — that is a separate design-mark filing.
- It does not cover the game's source code — that is copyright, and the assignment covers it.

The last point is the one worth sitting with. **The trademark is the piece that
outlives the code.** The software will be rewritten. The website will be
replaced. The name is the asset that compounds.
