## 1. SML Agent Role & Responsibilities

### 1.1 Who Are SML Agents?

SML Agents are the human face of Self-Made Legends. You are trusted representatives who:
- Build customer trust and confidence
- Explain SML products and process
- Guide customers through the application
- Coordinate with AI bots and backend systems
- Escalate issues appropriately
- Maintain data integrity and privacy
- Represent the SML brand and mission

### 1.2 Core Agent Responsibilities

**Daily Responsibilities:**
1. ✅ Handle inbound customer inquiries (email, phone, chat)
2. ✅ Follow up on quote requests and applications
3. ✅ Explain Final Expense insurance and SML process
4. ✅ Guide customers through underwriting
5. ✅ Review AI bot recommendations
6. ✅ Prepare applications for carrier submission
7. ✅ Respond to customer questions with clarity
8. ✅ Log all interactions accurately
9. ✅ Maintain data confidentiality
10. ✅ Identify and escalate problems

**Weekly Responsibilities:**
- [ ] Team coordination and standup
- [ ] Compliance check-in
- [ ] Quality metrics review
- [ ] Customer feedback assessment
- [ ] Team collaboration

**Monthly Responsibilities:**
- [ ] Performance review
- [ ] Training updates
- [ ] Process improvement suggestions
- [ ] Goal assessment

### 1.3 Agent Authority & Boundaries

**What Agents CAN Do:**
✅ Answer customer questions  
✅ Explain SML process  
✅ Guide through application  
✅ Provide status updates  
✅ Escalate to manager  
✅ Offer assistance and support  
✅ Make customers feel valued  

**What Agents CANNOT Do:**
❌ Make final approval decisions  
❌ Guarantee coverage or pricing  
❌ Override underwriting rules  
❌ Make exceptions to policy  
❌ Share customer data inappropriately  
❌ Promise specific outcomes  
❌ Make medical or legal determinations

## 2. SML Brand & Communication Standards

### 2.1 The SML Tone (How You Speak)

Every interaction should reflect four key qualities:

**🤝 WARM**
- Empathetic and caring
- Genuinely interested in helping
- Acknowledges customer feelings
- Takes time for each person

**🎯 CLEAR**
- Uses simple, plain language
- Explains concepts thoroughly
- No jargon or technical terms
- Breaks down complex ideas

**🛡️ TRUSTWORTHY**
- Honest and transparent
- Doesn't promise what you can't deliver
- Admits when you don't know something
- Follows through on commitments

**💬 MODERN**
- Conversational style
- Uses natural language
- Relates to customers as real people
- Stays current and relevant

### 2.2 SML Communication Framework

**When Answering Any Question, Use This Framework:**

1. **Acknowledge:** Recognize their question or concern
2. **Explain:** Provide clear, simple answer
3. **Reassure:** Help them feel confident
4. **Next Step:** Tell them what happens next

**Example:**
```
Customer: "What if I get denied?"

Agent: "That's a good concern—let me explain how that works.

After you submit your application, our team and our partner 
carrier review everything. Most people get approved, but if 
the carrier decides it's not a good fit, they'll let you know 
and explain why.

If that happens, you can ask questions and understand the 
reason. We'll also help you explore other options if needed.

But most people don't have to worry—we work with carriers 
that are willing to work with people in various health 
situations.

Does that help? Any other questions?"
```

### 2.3 Prohibited Language (Never Say These)

❌ "We guarantee approval"  
❌ "Everyone gets approved"  
❌ "You're definitely approved"  
❌ "This is definitely covered"  
❌ "You shouldn't have any problems"  
❌ "We never deny anyone"  
❌ "Don't worry, it's guaranteed"  
❌ "This is the best deal out there"  
❌ "No one else offers this"  
❌ "If you don't do this now, you'll regret it"  

### 2.4 Required Clarifications (When Relevant)

✅ "SML provides quotes; a licensed carrier issues the policy"  
✅ "I can't guarantee approval—the carrier makes that decision"  
✅ "Final pricing is determined during the carrier's review"  
✅ "This is a quote, not a final approval"  
✅ "Your information must be accurate and complete"  
✅ "See our Terms & Conditions for full details"

## 3. Handling Inbound Leads

### 3.1 Lead Intake Process

**When Lead Comes In (Email, Phone, Chat, Form):**

**Step 1: Acknowledge Immediately**
- Email: Respond within 2 hours
- Phone: Answer within 3 rings or return call within 1 hour
- Chat: Connect within 5 minutes during business hours

**Step 2: Greet Warmly**
```
"Hi [Name]! Thanks for reaching out to SML. 
I'm [Your Name]. How can I help you today?"
```

**Step 3: Understand Their Situation**
- Listen to what they need
- Ask clarifying questions
- Don't interrupt
- Take notes

**Step 4: Offer Solution**
```
"It sounds like you're interested in getting a quote. 
I can help you with that. We can do this two ways:

1. I can have you answer 6 quick questions online 
   right now—takes about 2 minutes
2. I can ask you the questions myself if you prefer

Which works better for you?"
```

**Step 5: Move Forward**
- Get them to quote submission
- Answer initial questions
- Schedule follow-up if needed

### 3.2 Lead Sources

Be aware of where leads come from:
- **Direct Quote Form:** Customer filled 6-question form
- **Agent Bot Chat:** Customer used AI chatbot
- **Funeral Home Referral:** Family scanned QR code
- **Direct Phone/Email:** Customer called or emailed
- **Partner Referral:** Community partner referred

**Always:**
- Log the source in CRM
- Thank referral sources
- Track for commission purposes

### 3.3 Lead Quality Check

Before proceeding, verify:
- ✅ All 6 required fields present
- ✅ Age within range (40-85)
- ✅ Contact information valid
- ✅ Basic information reasonable
- ✅ No immediate red flags

If information seems incomplete or unusual:
- Contact customer for clarification
- Verify information
- Escalate if concerns

## 4. Managing Quote Requests

### 4.1 Quote Follow-Up Timeline

**Within 24 hours:**
- [ ] Quote submitted and stored
- [ ] Underwriting Bot analysis complete
- [ ] Agent reviews recommendation
- [ ] Initial contact attempt made

**Within 48 hours:**
- [ ] Customer contacted
- [ ] Next steps explained
- [ ] Questions answered
- [ ] Status documented

**Within 5 business days:**
- [ ] Quote status updated
- [ ] If approved, carrier contacted
- [ ] If pending, additional info requested
- [ ] Customer updated

### 4.2 Quote Review Workflow

**When Quote Comes In:**

1. **Pull Up Complete Quote**
   - Customer data (all 6 fields)
   - Timestamp and source
   - Any notes from submission

2. **Review Underwriting Bot Analysis**
   - Risk score (0-100)
   - Recommendation
   - Any flags or concerns
   - Confidence level

3. **Assess Data Quality**
   - Is information complete?
   - Are there any inconsistencies?
   - Does anything seem off?
   - Any clarifications needed?

4. **Make Decision**
   - Approve for carrier submission? (Usually scores 0-60)
   - Request more information? (Scores 31-80)
   - Escalate to manager? (Scores 81-100 or concerns)
   - Flag for fraud review? (If suspicious)

5. **Document Decision**
   - Log in system
   - Note reason for decision
   - Schedule next action
   - Set timeline

### 4.3 Quote Status Communication

**Email to Customer (Example):**
```
Subject: Your SML Quote is Ready

Hi [Name],

Thank you for choosing SML! I've reviewed your information, 
and I want to reach out personally to walk you through 
what's next.

The great news: Based on the information you provided, 
you're a great fit for our program, and we're moving forward 
with our partner insurance carriers.

Here's what happens next:
1. We submit your information to our carriers
2. They review your application (usually 3-5 business days)
3. You'll hear from us with their decision
4. If approved, we'll explain your policy options

A couple of things I want to clarify:
- [If applicable] Can you confirm your health history?
- Is [phone number] the best way to reach you?

Feel free to call me at [number] if you have any questions. 
I'm here to help!

Best,
[Your Name]
SML Team
```

## 5. Explaining Final Expense Insurance

### 5.1 What Is Final Expense Insurance?

When customers ask, explain clearly:

```
"Final Expense insurance is a type of life insurance 
designed specifically to cover the costs that come up 
when someone passes away.

Think of it this way: When a loved one dies, there are 
costs—funeral expenses, hospital bills, maybe outstanding 
debts. Final Expense insurance provides money to cover 
those costs so your family doesn't have to struggle 
financially during an already difficult time.

With SML, you get:
- Coverage from $5,000 to $25,000
- Quick approval (most within 3-5 days)
- No medical exam required
- Simple process—just answer 6 questions

Does that help? Any questions about how it works?"
```

### 5.2 Common Questions & Answers

**Q: Who needs Final Expense insurance?**
```
"Anyone who wants to make sure their family won't be 
burdened financially when they pass away. Many people 
use it to cover funeral costs (typically $7,000-$15,000), 
medical bills, or other debts. It's especially important 
if your family depends on you financially."
```

**Q: Why is there no medical exam?**
```
"Great question. SML believes in transparency, not 
testing. We ask you direct questions about your health, 
and we trust you to be honest. If you have health 
conditions, that's okay—we work with carriers that can 
help people in various health situations."
```

**Q: What if I have a health condition?**
```
"Health conditions don't automatically disqualify you. 
We have carriers who work with people who have diabetes, 
hypertension, heart conditions, and more. The key is 
being honest about your health. We'll get you a quote 
and work with the best carrier for your situation."
```

**Q: How much does it cost?**
```
"The cost varies based on your age, health, and the 
coverage amount you choose. For example, a 60-year-old 
non-smoker might pay $15-30 per month for $10,000 in 
coverage. But the best way to know is to get a free quote. 
Want to answer a few quick questions so I can show you?"
```

**Q: What if I get denied?**
```
"Most people don't get denied. But if for some reason 
a carrier decides it's not a fit, they'll explain why. 
We also work with multiple carriers, so we have options. 
You won't be stuck."
```

### 5.3 Addressing Objections

**"I don't want to think about death"**
```
"I totally understand. It's not a fun topic. But here's 
the thing: Having this coverage means your family won't 
have to figure out finances during the hardest time of 
their lives. It's actually a gift to them. Plus, once 
you have it, you don't have to think about it anymore."
```

**"It's probably expensive"**
```
"It's actually more affordable than most people think. 
Final Expense is designed to be economical. Let me show 
you a quote—you might be surprised at how reasonable it is."
```

**"I'm too old/unhealthy"**
```
"That's exactly why Final Expense insurance exists—for 
people in various life stages and health situations. Our 
carriers understand that. Let's see if we can get you a 
quote. The worst that happens is we know more about your 
options."
```

## 6. Guiding Customers Through Application

### 6.1 Application Steps

**Step 1: Introduction (Setting Expectations)**
```
"Let me walk you through the application. It's really 
simple—just 6 questions about your age, health, and 
what coverage you need.

We've designed it to be quick (about 2 minutes) and 
straightforward. All the information you provide is 
confidential and secure.

Ready?"
```

**Step 2: Question-by-Question Guidance**

For each question, explain why it matters:

```
Age: "First, how old are you?"
(Why: This helps us understand your timeline and 
affects the coverage options available to you.)

Smoking: "Do you currently smoke?"
(Why: Smoking status can affect your rates, so we need 
to know for accurate pricing.)

Health: "Do you have any major health conditions?"
(Why: Understanding your health helps us find the right 
carrier match for your situation.)

Coverage: "How much coverage do you want?"
(Why: This determines how much protection your family 
will have and affects your monthly cost.)

Contact: "Finally, what's the best way to reach you?"
(Why: So we can follow up with your quote and answer 
any other questions.)
```

**Step 3: Confirmation (Verify Accuracy)**
```
"Perfect. Let me just make sure I have everything right:

Age: 65
Smoking: No
Health: Diabetes
Coverage: $15,000
Contact: john@email.com, 555-1234

Is that all correct? Anything I should change?"
```

**Step 4: Next Steps (Set Expectations)**
```
"Excellent. Your information is submitted. Here's what 
happens next:

1. Our AI team analyzes your application (happens 
   immediately)
2. I review it personally
3. We submit it to the best-fit carrier for your 
   situation
4. The carrier typically responds within 3-5 business days
5. We'll call you as soon as we hear back

You'll also get an email confirmation with your 
application number so you can check the status anytime.

Questions before we wrap up?"
```

### 6.2 Common Application Issues

**Applicant Age Outside Range (Too Young or Too Old)**
```
Agent: "I want to make sure I have this right. Did you 
say you're 35?"

Customer: "Yes."

Agent: "Thank you for clarifying. Our Final Expense 
insurance is designed for ages 40-85. You're a bit young 
for this product, but let me tell you about some other 
options that might work better for you..."

[Suggest alternative products or features]
```

**Missing or Incomplete Information**
```
Agent: "I notice you didn't specify a health condition. 
Do you have any major health issues we should know about? 
You can say 'none' if that's the case."

Customer: "I have diabetes and some heart issues."

Agent: "Thank you for being honest about that. That's 
helpful information. Let me make sure I get this right—
diabetes and heart condition. Anything else?"
```

**Customer Hesitation or Concerns**
```
Agent: "I'm sensing you might have some concerns. 
What's on your mind?"

Customer: "I'm worried about whether I'll be approved."

Agent: "That's a fair concern. Let me be honest with you. 
Most people get approved. We have multiple carriers, and 
we're good at matching people with the right one for 
their situation. Would you feel better if we went ahead 
and saw what they say?"
```

## 7. Using Backend Data & Bot Recommendations

### 7.1 Accessing Backend Information

**How to Review a Quote:**

1. **Log In to CRM/System**
   - Navigate to Leads section
   - Search by customer name or lead ID
   - Pull up customer record

2. **View Customer Data**
   - See all 6 fields (age, gender, smoking, health, coverage, contact)
   - Review timestamp (when submitted)
   - Check source (where they came from)
   - Note any additional information captured

3. **Review Bot Analysis**
   - Click "Underwriting Bot Recommendation"
   - Review risk score (0-100)
   - Read recommendation text
   - Check confidence level
   - Note any flags or concerns

4. **Assess Data Quality**
   - Is information complete?
   - Does it seem accurate?
   - Any inconsistencies?
   - Any data quality issues?

### 7.2 Understanding Bot Recommendations

**Risk Score Interpretation:**

| Score | Category | Typical Action |
|-------|----------|---|
| 0-30 | LOW RISK | Approve immediately |
| 31-60 | MODERATE RISK | Approve with standard conditions |
| 61-80 | ELEVATED RISK | Request more info or manager review |
| 81-100 | HIGH RISK | Escalate to manager or detailed review |

**Reading Bot Output:**

The bot provides:
- ✅ Risk score with breakdown
- ✅ Recommendation (Approve/Request Info/Escalate)
- ✅ Reasoning for recommendation
- ✅ Any flags or concerns
- ✅ Confidence level (how sure the bot is)

**Using the Recommendation:**

```
Bot Says: "Approve with standard conditions"
(Risk Score: 52)

Agent Interpretation: "This customer is moderately low 
risk. The underwriting bot recommends approval with 
standard terms. I should:
1. Contact customer
2. Confirm information
3. Explain what's next
4. Submit to carrier
5. Inform customer of expected timeline"
```

### 7.3 When to Disagree with Bot Recommendation

You can override a bot recommendation if:
- **Data Quality Issue:** You verify information is inaccurate or incomplete
- **Context the Bot Missed:** You know something about the customer situation the bot doesn't
- **Red Flag:** You spot fraud or concern not flagged by bot
- **Manager Direction:** Your manager directs a different action

**If You Disagree:**
1. Document your concern
2. Note what the bot missed or misunderstood
3. Escalate to manager
4. Explain your reasoning
5. Let manager make final decision

**Example:**
```
Bot Recommendation: "Approve"
Your Concern: Customer said age 65, but on the phone 
they mentioned being retired for 20 years (would be ~87)

Action: Call customer to verify age. If 87, escalate to 
manager even though bot approved.
```

## 8. Handling Underwriting Recommendations

### 8.1 Communicating Underwriting Decision to Customer

**Scenario 1: Approved for Carrier Submission**

```
Agent calls customer:

"Hi [Name], I have good news. I've reviewed your 
application, and it looks great. We're moving forward 
with submitting your information to our partner 
insurance carriers.

Here's what this means:
1. We're sending your details to carriers today
2. They'll do their own review (usually 3-5 days)
3. If approved, they'll send us details about your 
   policy and rates
4. We'll call you with the final offer

You should expect to hear from me by [specific date] 
with an update. Any questions?"
```

**Scenario 2: Need More Information**

```
Agent calls customer:

"Hi [Name], thanks for submitting your application. 
I've reviewed it, and I just want to clarify a couple 
of things to make sure we have everything right.

You mentioned you have diabetes—can I ask:
1. How long have you had it?
2. How is it managed? (Medication, lifestyle changes?)
3. Any recent complications?

These details help us match you with the right carrier. 
Once I have this info, we'll move forward."
```

**Scenario 3: Escalation to Manager**

```
Agent (to customer):

"Hi [Name], thanks for your application. I've reviewed 
it, and I think it's best if I have one of our managers 
take a deeper look. Nothing to worry about—it's just 
our process for making sure we handle everything right.

A manager will call you within 24 hours to discuss 
your application. Okay?"

[Then escalate to manager with full notes]
```

### 8.2 Setting Customer Expectations

Always set clear expectations:

```
"Here's our timeline:
• Today: I submit your info to carriers
• Tomorrow-Friday: Carriers review (3-5 business days)
• By [DATE]: I call you with news

If we haven't heard anything by Friday, I'll follow up 
with the carriers. You don't need to do anything—I'll 
keep you in the loop."
```

## 9. Managing Claims Inquiries

### 9.1 Claims Department Responsibility

When customer calls about a claim:

**Step 1: Listen & Acknowledge**
```
"I'm so sorry for your loss. I understand you need to 
file a claim. I'm going to help you through this process.

Can you tell me the name of your loved one?"
```

**Step 2: Get Basic Information**
- Deceased name
- Date of death
- Policy number (if available)
- Beneficiary contact info
- Relationship to deceased

**Step 3: Explain Process**
```
"Here's how the claim process works:

1. I'm going to connect you with our Claims Bot, which 
   will walk you through what documents we need
2. Once you provide those documents, our claims team 
   processes everything
3. We send it to the insurance carrier
4. The carrier processes and issues payment

It typically takes 10-15 business days from when we 
have all documents."
```

**Step 4: Route to Claims Bot**
```
"I'm going to connect you with our Claims Bot now. 
It will guide you step-by-step through gathering 
documents. You don't have to memorize anything—it'll 
explain everything.

This might be difficult, and that's okay. The bot is 
programmed to be understanding. Ready?"
```

**Step 5: Follow Up**
- Mark in system for follow-up
- Assign to claims specialist
- Document all information
- Provide timeline update

### 9.2 Claims Best Practices

**DO:**
✅ Treat with utmost compassion  
✅ Move quickly  
✅ Explain clearly  
✅ Be available  
✅ Follow up regularly  
✅ Provide resources if needed  

**DON'T:**
❌ Rush the process  
❌ Use cold or formal language  
❌ Make assumptions  
❌ Promise specific amounts  
❌ Delay documentation requests  
❌ Make the family repeat information

## 10. Escalating Issues

### 10.1 When to Escalate

**Escalate to Manager:**
- High-risk application (score 81-100)
- Potential fraud concerns
- Customer complaints or dissatisfaction
- Complex health situations
- Data quality questions
- Unclear bot recommendation
- Anything uncertain

**Escalate to Legal:**
- Potential legal issues
- Regulatory questions
- Compliance concerns
- Fraud investigations
- Policy interpretation

**Escalate to Compliance:**
- Communication violations
- Prohibited language used
- Privacy concerns
- Data breach
- Compliance questions

### 10.2 Escalation Process

**Step 1: Document Everything**
- What's the issue?
- What's been done so far?
- Why are you escalating?
- What's the urgency?

**Step 2: Contact Appropriate Person**
- Manager for operational issues
- Legal for legal questions
- Compliance for compliance issues
- Include all relevant information

**Step 3: Follow Up**
- Check on status
- Keep customer informed
- Document outcome
- Learn for future

**Example Escalation Note:**
```
ESCALATION ALERT

Issue: Potential fraud
Applicant: Jane Smith (lead_1694540450_x2y8z3)
Problem: Multiple applications with same phone number 
but different names and ages this week
Documentation: All applications in system
Risk Level: HIGH
Action Needed: Fraud investigation and customer contact
Assigned To: Fraud Analyst
Timeline: Within 24 hours
```

## 11. Logging Interactions & Records

### 11.1 Documentation Requirements

Every customer interaction must be logged:

**What to Log:**
- ✅ Date and time of interaction
- ✅ Contact method (phone, email, chat)
- ✅ Customer name and ID
- ✅ What was discussed
- ✅ Action taken
- ✅ Next steps
- ✅ Any issues or flags

**Where to Log:**
- CRM system (primary)
- Lead record (interaction history)
- Any special notes for team

**How to Log:**
Be clear, concise, and specific.

**Good Example:**
```
2026-09-12 2:30 PM - Phone Call
Customer: John Davis (lead_1694540400_a7x9k2)
Topic: Follow-up on quote (submitted 2026-09-11)
Discussion: 
- Reviewed bot recommendation (score 52, Approve)
- Explained next steps (carrier submission)
- Answered health condition questions
- Confirmed contact info is correct

Action Taken:
- Submitted to Blue Cross carrier
- Explained 3-5 day timeline
- Promised follow-up by 2026-09-17

Next Steps:
- Follow up with carrier if no response by 2026-09-17
- Call customer back with news

Notes: Customer seemed relieved and satisfied.
```

**Bad Example:**
```
Called customer. Discussed application. Going to submit.
```

### 11.2 Data Accuracy

**Ensure All Data Is:**
- ✅ Accurate (double-check before logging)
- ✅ Complete (include all relevant info)
- ✅ Legible (clear and easy to read)
- ✅ Timely (log immediately after interaction)
- ✅ Confidential (store securely)

**Red Flags for Bad Data:**
❌ Spelling errors in customer name  
❌ Wrong phone number  
❌ Incomplete information  
❌ Conflicting information  
❌ Notes weeks after interaction

## 13. Data Protection & Privacy

You handle sensitive information:
- Name and contact details
- Age and health information
- Financial situations
- Family circumstances

**Golden Rule: Treat data like it's your own family's information.**

### 13.2 Data Protection Practices

**DO:**
✅ Keep data confidential  
✅ Access only what you need  
✅ Store securely in SML systems  
✅ Use strong passwords  
✅ Log out when leaving desk  
✅ Use VPN on public wifi  
✅ Encrypt sensitive communications  
✅ Follow company data policy  

**DON'T:**
❌ Share customer info with anyone  
❌ Write down passwords  
❌ Use public wifi for customer data  
❌ Take screenshots of customer data  
❌ Leave customer files visible

## 14. Customer Satisfaction Standards

### 14.1 What Great Customer Service Looks Like

**Elements of Excellent Service:**
1. **Responsiveness:** Quick replies and callbacks
2. **Clarity:** Simple explanations, no jargon
3. **Empathy:** Understanding their situation
4. **Professionalism:** Courteous, respectful tone
5. **Reliability:** Following through on promises
6. **Problem-solving:** Helping find solutions
7. **Transparency:** Being honest about options

### 14.2 Customer Satisfaction Checkpoints

**After Initial Contact:**
- Did customer understand what SML does?
- Do they feel comfortable moving forward?
- Do they have questions answered?

**After Quote Submission:**
- Did customer feel heard and understood?
- Are they confident in next steps?
- Do they know when to expect contact?

**After Carrier Submission:**
- Does customer understand timeline?
- Do they know how to reach us?
- Do they feel confident about outcome?

**After Policy Issue or Claim:**
- Is customer satisfied with service?
- Would they recommend SML?
- Any feedback for improvement?

### 14.3 Handling Unhappy Customers

**If Customer is Frustrated:**

```
1. Listen without interrupting
2. Apologize for the frustration (even if not your fault)
3. Explain what happened clearly
4. Take responsibility ("Here's what I'll do...")
5. Offer solution
6. Follow through
7. Follow up to confirm satisfaction

Example:
"I hear that you're frustrated, and I'm sorry about that. 
Let me be honest about what happened and what I'm going to 
do to fix it. [Explain clearly] Here's my plan to make this 
right: [Solution] You'll hear from me by [date] with an update. 
Fair enough?"
```
