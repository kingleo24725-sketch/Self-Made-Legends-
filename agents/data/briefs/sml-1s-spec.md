# SML 1s — first footwear specification

**SML-1S-001** · Draft 1 · 27 August 2026

From the second round of mockups: the court low in black/gold and white/gold.
Chosen over the runners because the court low is the most generic form in
footwear, which makes it the safest place to put an identity of your own — and
because black/gold and white/gold are the two colourways that carry the house.

**This is a draft with unknowns in it, deliberately.** Every `UNKNOWN` is
something a factory would otherwise guess at, and every one of them is in §9.

**Silhouette described, never borrowed:** low-cut court profile with a full
wrapped foxing tape, suede toe overlay, five-eyelet eyestay, external heel
counter, padded collar, and a single diagonal band across the lateral quarter.
No element of this is taken from another house's model, and the diagonal band
sits where a brand identifier goes — which is the point. Over time it becomes
the house's own mark.

**Colour standards:** `UNKNOWN — Pantone TCX required on every piece.` Hex
values exist (`#0B0C11` Onyx, `#E7E1D4` Bone, `#C9A227` gold) but a hex is not
a colour standard. A tannery matches to a physical swatch.

**The metallic gold band is the hardest material in this spec.** Metallic
finishes on leather crack at the flex point. Ask each factory for a flex-tested
metallic they already run rather than specifying one — this is the single most
likely thing to fail a wear test.

## 6. Branding

| Mark | Placement | From landmark | Size | Method | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SML` script | Lateral quarter | 42mm forward of heel seam, 30mm up from foxing | 62 × 26mm | 3D moulded applique, stitched | Gold |
| Crown | Medial + lateral heel | 12mm below topline, centred on heel seam | 18 × 12mm | Debossed and gold-foiled | Gold |
| `SELF-MADE LEGENDS` `EST. MMXXVI` | Medial quarter | 38mm forward of heel seam | 46mm wide, 3 lines | Debossed and gold-foiled | Gold |
| Star | Midsole, medial, forefoot | 90mm forward of heel | 9mm | Foil on foxing | Gold |
| Tongue label | Tongue centre | 20mm below top edge | 30 × 16mm | Woven, satin | Gold on black |
| Insole | Full print | | | Heat transfer | Crest + numbering |
| Outsole | Forefoot | | | Moulded | `SELF-MADE LEGENDS` |

**Deliberately not embroidery.** The needle perforates hide and it does not
heal, so fine gold thread on leather tears at the flex point. The script goes
on as a **3D moulded applique with a stitched edge** — which is what gives it
the raised gold look in the render — and the small type is **debossed and
foiled**, which holds at 4mm where thread would not.

This is the opposite rule from the apparel line, where gold must be thread. On
leather, thread is the wrong answer.

## 8. Quality standard

- Sole-to-upper peel: minimum 3.0 kg/cm
- Flex: 30,000 cycles, no cracking at the foxing or the gold band
- **Metallic band flex-tested separately** — this is where it will fail
- Colour fastness to rubbing, dry and wet
- No visible adhesive at the foxing line
- Left and right within 2mm on every measured point
- Gold foil intact after 5,000 abrasion cycles

| Sample cost | $300–800, and budget for 3 rounds |
| Tooling | **$0** on a stock sole |
| Sample lead time | 4–6 weeks |
| Bulk lead time | 60–90 days |

**Two colourways at 250 pairs each is roughly $20–26k of stock before a single
pair sells.** That is the real gate on footwear, and no amount of design work
moves it.

---

## 11. What to actually do next

1. **Do not commission anything yet.** Sell the hoodie first.
2. Send this spec to **three court-shoe factories** and ask four questions:
   what stock last and cupsole do you hold, what is your MOQ, what is your
   sample cost, and can you run a flex-tested metallic gold. Their answers cost
   you nothing and will teach you more than another round of renders.
3. Get the silhouette and the diagonal band cleared by a footwear IP attorney
   **before** any tooling money moves.
4. Only then, sample.

> **The renders cannot go to a factory and cannot be sold as product
> photography.** They are AI-generated, so no human authorship and no US
> copyright, and no factory can build from a picture. What they are genuinely
> good for is showing a factory what you mean, and testing demand on the
> newsletter before you spend $20k — clearly labelled as concepts, because a
> render presented as a photograph of a real shoe is a claim you cannot back.
