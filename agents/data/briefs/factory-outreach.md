# Factory outreach — copy, paste, send

The gap between where you are and a real shoe is one email to three factories.
It costs nothing, and their four answers will teach you more than another
round of renders.

---

## The email

Send it to three. Not one — you need to compare, and the first quote always
looks reasonable until you see the second.

> **Subject:** Court low / retro runner — new US brand, 250–500 pairs, stock last
>
> Hello,
>
> I'm Jason Brown, founder of Self-Made Legends LLC, a streetwear brand in
> Missouri. We're launching our first footwear style and looking for a
> production partner.
>
> **What we need**
>
> A low-cut leather trainer — one style, two colourways, black/gold and
> white/gold. Full-grain leather upper, suede toe overlay, cupsole with a
> wrapped foxing tape, metallic gold detailing.
>
> **We want to use one of your existing lasts and sole units.** We're not
> asking you to tool anything for a first season. Our branding goes on the
> upper, the foxing and the insole — the shape is yours.
>
> **Four questions, and short answers are fine:**
>
> 1. Which court or retro lasts and cupsoles do you already hold that we could
>    build on? Can you send photos or a sample?
> 2. What's your minimum order per colourway at this spec?
> 3. What does a first sample cost, and how long does it take?
> 4. Can you run a **flex-tested metallic gold** leather or PU? We need a gold
>    band that won't crack at the flex point, and we'd rather use something you
>    already run than specify one blind.
>
> I've attached our draft specification. It has open questions in it on
> purpose — I'd rather you tell me than guess.
>
> Thank you,
>
> Jason D Brown Jr
> Self-Made Legends LLC · Missouri, United States
> selfmadelegendsz.com

**Attach:** `agents/data/briefs/regent-low-spec.md`

## Where to find them

| Where | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Alibaba / Made-in-China** | Filter for footwear, then message the ones with real factory photos rather than a stock-image storefront. Free. |
| **Sourcify, Maker's Row, Pietra** | Sourcing agents. They charge, and they filter out the time-wasters. |
| **A US footwear developer** | Costs more per hour, saves you months. Search "footwear product development consultant". |
| **The Footwear Distributors and Retailers of America** | Trade body. Their member list is real companies. |

**Portugal, Spain and Italy** for leather at a premium price. **Vietnam,
Indonesia and China** for volume. **Mexico** if you want shorter shipping into
the US and lower duty exposure.

## Reading their answers

| They say | It means |
|---|---|
| "MOQ 2,000 pairs" | Too big for you. Thank them and move on. |
| "MOQ 100–300" | This is your range. Keep talking. |
| "We can make any design you send" | Careful. A factory that never pushes back is one that will build your mistakes exactly. |
| "Sample $150" | Suspiciously cheap. Ask what it includes. |
| "Sample $400–800, 2–3 rounds" | Normal and honest. |
| "We'll need to tool a mould, $5,000" | You asked for a stock sole. Ask again, or move on. |
| No answer in 5 working days | Move on. Responsiveness now is responsiveness later. |

## Before you send money

- [ ] Get the **physical sample** in your hands. Never approve from a photo.
- [ ] Wear them for a week. Flex the gold band deliberately and see if it cracks.
- [ ] Confirm they'll hold your patterns and **not sell your design to anyone else** — get it in writing.
- [ ] Confirm who owns the pattern. It should be you.
- [ ] Have a footwear IP attorney look at the chosen silhouette **before** bulk.
- [ ] Never pay 100% up front. 30% deposit / 70% on inspection is standard.

---

## What not to do

**Do not send a factory an AI render and ask them to make it.** They will
either say yes and build something else, or reverse-engineer the shoe the
render is actually copying — and then it's your name on a counterfeit, not
theirs.

Send the spec. The render can go in the same email as *"this is the look we're
after"*, clearly labelled a concept.

---

## And the thing that keeps happening

Three rounds of mockups have now produced Air Force 1s, an Air Griffey, and a
Cortez. That is not a prompting problem you can fix with better wording.

**The generator was trained on real sneakers.** Ask it for a sneaker and it
returns the sneakers it knows, redressed. It will do it again on round four.

So use it for what it is good at — **colourways on a shape you already own** —
and get the shape from a factory's own library, where it is already made,
already cleared, and belongs to nobody's trademark.

That is not a compromise. It is how nearly every small footwear brand on earth
has started.
